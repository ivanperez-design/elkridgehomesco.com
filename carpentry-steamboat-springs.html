How the work is built

Carpentry you can inspect.

Decks that carry a Steamboat winter. Footings to the 48-inch frost depth, joist tape on every joist, fasteners rated for the decking, and the ledger flashed so water stays off the house.
Finish work that reads consistent through the whole house. Base, casing, doors, stair detail, and built-ins set true, so reveals and miters match from room to room.
Framing sized to your engineered load, not to a flatland span table — square, plumb, and tied into the existing structure.

Steamboat specifics

What a Steamboat deck has to survive.

Ground snow load verified for your parcel before we size a single beam — Routt is a case-study zone, so a regional number isn't good enough.
Footings to 48 inches, the countywide frost depth, so the deck doesn't heave.
Decking checked against your parcel's wildfire classification before we order — some product lines don't qualify on the higher classes.
Permit and setback checked against the parcel before design gets far.
Asbestos and lead tested before demolition on any structure built before 1980.
Hidden fasteners and joist tape as standard, not an upgrade.
Questions we get

Three things callers ask first.

Do you take small carpentry jobs or repair lists?
No. We take on project-scale carpentry — decks, built-ins and stairs, trim packages, and the framing for additions — so the owner can run every job personally. If you have a repair list, the Routt County building department keeps a listing of local contractors.
Can you build a deck that holds Steamboat snow?
Yes. Every deck is sized to the ground snow load verified for your parcel, on footings to the 48-inch frost depth, with the water kept off your house. A written scope shows exactly what is being built before we start.
Do you do just the framing for an addition, or the whole addition?
Either. We frame additions and structural changes on their own, and we take on the whole addition — foundation to finish — as one written scope with one owner accountable.
