Steamboat specifics

Built to the mountain, not to flatland specs.

Ground snow load verified per parcel, never a regional number.
Footings taken to the countywide 48-inch frost depth.
A Climate Zone 7B envelope — continuous exterior insulation and triple-pane windows preferred at this altitude.
Wildfire-zone requirements checked where your parcel is mapped — roofing and siding chosen against the parcel's classification, not a guess.
Pre-1980 structures tested for asbestos and lead before any demolition on an addition or a rebuild.
Subs booked three to four weeks ahead through the summer, because a short building season is the schedule risk up here.
Questions we get

Three things owners ask first.

Do you build from an architect's plans, or do you design too?
Both paths work. If you have a full set from an architect, we build from it and bring our pre-construction questions to the table early. If you are starting from a concept, we help you get to a buildable set with a written scope before ground breaks.
We live out of town. How does a custom build work?
One owner as your single point of contact, a photo and status update every week, a written weekly recap, and selections handled by video and email. Both decision-makers are on every message. Most out-of-town owners walk the finished house, not the half-built one.
Do you take on major additions, not just ground-up homes?
Yes. Primary-suite and great-room expansions, second stories, garages, and accessory dwelling units where the lot and local code allow — new foundation to frost depth, tied into the existing structure so it reads as one home.

Required disclosure (Colorado CRS §38-22-101): Any person or company supplying labor or materials for this improvement to your property may file a lien against your property if that person or company is not paid for the contributions.
